# MTJR — Competitive Landscape

*Compiled 2026-09-01 from web research. Figures are as reported; verify before citing.*

## TL;DR

The "digitize informal merchants" space is **crowded, capital-scarred, and consolidating**.
The consumer marketplace layer in Egypt is **anchored by Amazon.eg and Noon** and fragmenting.
The pure-connector, demand-sensing angle is under-occupied — but only survives if it stays
asset-light. Nearly every player that went asset-heavy (own warehouses, own fleet, lending)
has shut down, merged, or pivoted to fintech.

## B2B / merchant-digitization players

### MaxAB-Wasoko (the 800-lb gorilla)
- Merger of MaxAB (Egypt/Morocco) + Wasoko (formerly Sokowatch, East Africa) completed 2024,
  deal valued ~$230M.
- Connects ~450,000 informal retailers to supply across Egypt, Kenya, Tanzania, Rwanda,
  Morocco. Scaled *down* from 8 markets to 5.
- **Pivoted from e-commerce to fintech** as the core business. Got an Egyptian financial
  services license (2025); fintech ~$180M annual turnover in Egypt. Wound down Morocco
  e-commerce.
- Acquired **Fatura** (2025) — 626 wholesalers across 16 Egyptian cities.
- Read: even the best-funded player concluded that pure B2B e-commerce economics don't work and
  the money is in payments/credit on top of the network.

### Cartona (Egypt) — the asset-light role model
- ~$22.1M raised total; $8.1M Series A extension July 2024 (Algebra Ventures, Silicon Badia,
  SANAD).
- ~188,000 retailers, 17 Egyptian cities. Reported **profitable / capital-efficient** via a
  pure-marketplace, no-inventory model (connects retailers to existing wholesalers/distributors).
- Read: this is the closest proof that a connector-only model *can* work in Egypt. It is also
  directly in MTJR's Phase 1/2 lane on the B2B side — study it hard, and differentiate on
  consumer-facing + demand intelligence, which Cartona does not emphasize.

### The graveyard (asset-heavy or "vitamin" plays)
- **Wabi, Zumi, MarketForce, Sokowatch (early e-comm), Joovlin (Nigeria, shut Jan 2025)** —
  all digitized informal retail, all folded or gutted. Common causes: asset-heavy warehousing/
  delivery, couldn't convert usage into revenue, unit economics in low-density markets.
- Investor mood since ~2023: fund B2B "painkillers" (non-negotiable problems), not consumer
  "vitamins" that burn capital.

## Consumer marketplace layer (Egypt)

- Market ~$9–10B in 2025, growing ~11–15%/yr; projected ~$14.5B by 2029.
- **Amazon.eg** (ex-Souq) — largest, ~11% of sales.
- **Noon** — fastest-growing major, ~8% share, expanding seller base and localized ops.
- Also: Jumia, B.TECH, Carrefour Egypt (omnichannel), Homzmart (furniture), Talabat (q-comm/
  grocery).
- Trend: **fragmentation** — no one dominates, category-specialist and omnichannel players
  gaining. A well-executed niche entrant is not obviously locked out.

## Visual / camera search (the later MTJR feature)

- Google Lens: ~15B visual searches/month in 2025, ~1 in 4 with commercial intent. Pinterest
  Lens ~600M/month.
- Only ~8% of e-commerce brands have on-site visual search; ~19% of US retailers adopted,
  ~40% plan to. Reported effects: +11% conversion, faster task completion, high repeat use.
- Read: real consumer pull, low competitor adoption regionally = a legitimate differentiator —
  but it's a Phase 2 feature, and it's now a solved engineering problem (image embeddings +
  vector search), not R&D.

## C2M / demand-sensing → manufacturing (the Phase 3 prize)

- Shein pioneered, Temu adopted: social listening + proprietary cart/behavior data → algorithm
  flags trends in days → small-batch runs of 100–500 units → scale only what sells. Sometimes
  production doesn't start until enough carts.
- Chinese marketplaces (Tmall, JD) run formal C2M programs with upstream factories.
- Read: this validates MTJR's end-game logic. The moat is the *proprietary demand data*, which
  is exactly what Phases 1–2 are designed to accumulate. Nobody is doing this bottom-up from
  digitized *local* merchants — that's the whitespace.

## Where MTJR could actually be differentiated

1. **Consumer-facing + merchant-digitizing at once** — B2B players (Cartona, MaxAB-Wasoko) are
   retailer-facing wholesale; consumer players (Amazon, Noon) don't digitize small merchants.
   MTJR sits in the gap.
2. **Demand intelligence as the stated product**, not an afterthought.
3. **Design/curation quality** on the consumer interface — cheap to do, rarely done well
   locally.
4. **Asset-light discipline** — the one trait shared by every survivor.

## Risks the landscape makes obvious

- Merchant digitization at scale needs field-agent capital MTJR won't have early → stay a
  hand-onboarded sliver until proven.
- MaxAB-Wasoko or Cartona could add a consumer + demand-data layer any time.
- "Connect only, transact off-platform" makes monetization and data capture harder — need a
  reason for both sides to keep the activity *on* the platform.
- Egypt macro (FX, inflation, consumer spending) has already stressed this whole sector.
